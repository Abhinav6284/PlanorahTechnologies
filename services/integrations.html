What we build

Most small companies do not have a data problem. They have a retyping problem. An order arrives on the website, someone copies it into a spreadsheet, someone else messages the customer on WhatsApp from a personal phone, and accounts hears about it on Friday. Every copy is a chance for the three systems to disagree, and by month-end they always do.

We build the connections that remove the retyping. A payment gateway that confirms the order the moment money is actually captured. A WhatsApp Business API sender that tells the customer what happened, from your verified business number rather than a staff member’s. An accounting sync that posts invoices and payments into Tally or Zoho Books on the schedule you choose. Where a system has a proper API we use it; where it does not, we use its import format and a scheduled job — and we tell you which of the two you are getting before you pay for it.

The part that decides whether any of this survives contact with a real month is what happens when a connection fails, because it will. Gateways have outages, tokens expire, a GST number gets rejected, an ERP is rebooted mid-sync. Every job we build retries with a back-off, records what it sent and what came back, and raises an alert to email or WhatsApp when it finally gives up. Failures become visible instead of silent — the difference between a two-minute fix and a reconciliation nightmare in the last week of the quarter.

Test keys first, live keys last.

Nothing touches real money or real customers until the same flow has run end to end in a sandbox. Four to five weeks for a typical set of three connections.

The ones worth asking first.

Ask anything else on WhatsApp or read the full FAQ.

Do we need the official WhatsApp Business API?

Only if your system needs to send messages on its own. If a person types every message, the free WhatsApp Business app is enough and we will say so rather than sell you an integration. The API is for order confirmations, reminders and status updates at volume, from a verified number, using templates Meta approves in advance. Meta charges per conversation and bills you directly, and template approval takes a few days — both are built into the quote and the schedule.

What happens when a payment succeeds but our site does not update?

That is the exact failure the design prevents. The gateway is the source of truth: we verify its signed callback and set the order from that, never from the browser the customer closed too early. Handlers are idempotent, so a repeated callback cannot create a second order or a second charge. If a callback never arrives at all, a daily reconciliation job compares the gateway’s settlement report against your orders and flags every difference by name and amount.

Can you connect to Tally? It runs on a PC in our office.

Usually yes, with a caveat worth saying before you pay anything. Tally Prime speaks XML over a local port on the machine it runs on, so a sync needs that machine switched on and reachable — either a connector service inside your network that pushes out, or a fixed link in. Zoho Books and most cloud ERPs are simpler because they have real APIs and are always on. We work out which category you fall into in the first conversation, and the quote says plainly what is required from your side.

What if a third-party API changes or breaks?

Providers do change things, sometimes with short notice. Every integration logs its requests and responses, so when something shifts we can see exactly what came back instead of guessing from a blank screen. The first 30 days of fixes are included. After that most clients keep a small monthly retainer for upkeep, or simply call us when something breaks — either is fine. The code, the keys and the documentation are yours, so another developer can pick it up without starting over.
