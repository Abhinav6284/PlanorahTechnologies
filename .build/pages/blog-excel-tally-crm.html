Six signs you have outgrown spreadsheets

Excel is a brilliant tool. The trouble starts when several people depend on the same sheet. If three or more of these sound familiar, it is time to look at a proper system:

  1. The same customer or product exists in three files, and they disagree.
  2. Staff phone or WhatsApp each other to ask whether stock is available or an order has gone out.
  3. Follow-ups on quotations depend on one person remembering.
  4. The monthly report takes someone two days to put together by hand.
  5. Nobody knows who changed a price, or when.
  6. When one key person is on leave, part of the business stops.

What a CRM actually does

CRM stands for customer relationship management, but for most businesses in Punjab it means something broader: one place where leads, quotations, orders, stock, payments due and staff tasks all live, with each person seeing the part they need.

  • One record per customer and product — edited once, seen everywhere.
  • Roles — the counter sees orders, accounts sees payments, the owner sees everything.
  • Alerts on WhatsApp and email — quotation sent, payment overdue, stock below reorder level — so people stop chasing each other.
  • An audit trail — who changed what, and when.
  • Reports that build themselves — and export to Excel whenever you want them.

Buy one, or have one built?

Be honest with yourself here. Off-the-shelf tools such as Zoho CRM are good, inexpensive, and often enough for a sales team that simply needs to track leads. If one of them fits how you work, buy it — it will cost less than having anything built.

Custom software makes sense when the off-the-shelf tools force your team to work around them — when your business has a process that is genuinely its own:

  • A manufacturer tracking orders through cutting, stitching and dispatch across two units
  • A wholesaler with dealer-specific price lists and credit limits
  • A clinic group with one calendar across branches and WhatsApp reminders
  • An institute running admissions from enquiry to enrolment with several counsellors

A custom system is built around how your counter, godown and office already work, so your team learns it in a day rather than changing how they work to suit the software.

Working alongside Tally

Most businesses keep Tally for accounts and want the new system to stay in step with it. That is usually possible, with one practical detail worth knowing before you pay for anything.

Tally Prime runs on a computer in your office and talks to other software through that machine. So a sync needs that computer switched on and reachable — either through a small connector running inside your office network, or a fixed connection in. Cloud accounting tools like Zoho Books are simpler, because they are always online and have proper connections built in.

Ask before you start: any good developer should tell you in the first conversation which of these applies to you, and the quote should say plainly what is needed from your side.

Moving your old data safely

Your existing Excel sheets and Tally exports are valuable. A careful migration looks like this:

  1. Collect the exports — Excel, CSV, Tally, or whatever your current system can produce.
  2. Map the columns to the new records — which column is the customer, the GST number, the credit limit.
  3. Run a duplicate and consistency check, and review it before anything is loaded.
  4. Get a list of rows that could not be matched, to decide on — nothing should be quietly dropped.
  5. Keep the old files untouched, so there is always something to compare against.

Then roll out to one team first — say the sales counter — for a week, fix what they find, and bring the rest of the business on after.

Common questions

Will my staff be able to use it?

If it is built around how they already work, yes — usually after a single training session. Training on handover day, a recorded walkthrough, and calls in Punjabi, Hindi or English all help.

Can I still get my data out into Excel?

You should always be able to. Every record should export to CSV or Excel on demand — data you cannot get out is data you do not really own.

Does it work on phones and tablets?

Yes. A web-based system works on a computer at the counter, a tablet in the godown and a phone in the field, with the screens arranged for each.
